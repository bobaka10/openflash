Install a custom Android ROM from your browser.

Pick your phone, pick a ROM, follow the steps. No command line and usually no extra software. Official and community ROMs are both supported, and each one is clearly labeled. Open source, and anyone can add a device on GitHub.

New to this? Read what a ROM, a bootloader and a recovery are, in plain words.

Works today: Google Pixel 4 with LineageOS, and Xiaomi Mi Pad 4 with PixelExperience Plus. Both were installed end to end on real devices, from Chrome on a computer. This is a young project and flashing erases your phone, so use it at your own risk. Every other device is a simulation and does not change your phone. Needs Chrome or Edge over https or localhost.

    Guides

    How to unlock the bootloader (Pixel)
    1. Settings → About phone → tap Build number 7 times.
    2. Settings → System → Developer options → turn on OEM unlocking and USB debugging.
    3. Power off, then hold Power + Volume Down to open the bootloader.
    4. Connect to the computer and confirm the unlock on the phone. This erases all data.

    Carrier-locked models (for example Verizon) usually cannot be unlocked. Check before you start.

    Brick and data-loss warning

    Flashing erases your phone and can make it unusable if it is interrupted or the wrong file is used. Back up your photos and files first, charge to at least 50%, use a good USB cable, and do not unplug during the process. You do this at your own risk.

    Official vs community builds

    Official builds are published by the ROM project itself for your exact device. Community builds are made by independent developers, often for devices the project does not support. They can be great, but they are harder to verify and less tested. Check who made the build and read its thread before installing.

    How your files are checked

    Before flashing, OpenFlash checks that each file has the right type, is not suspiciously small, starts with the right internal signature, and has your device codename in its name. If the download page shows a SHA-256 checksum, you can paste it and the file is compared exactly. Many builds do not publish one, so this is optional. Files never leave your computer. To get the checksum of a file on your computer, see How to check a SHA-256 checksum.

    Xiaomi Mi Pad 4 tips
    • Boot modes. Fastboot: hold Power + Volume Down until the word FASTBOOT appears. Recovery: hold Power + Volume Up, and when the Mi logo shows release Power but keep holding Volume Up until the menu appears.
    • Do not start Android between flashing the recovery and opening it for the first time. The original Android can replace the new recovery. That is why the installer does not restart the tablet by itself: you open the recovery by hand.
    • Only the PE recovery works with this ROM. TWRP and OrangeFox cannot install it. To switch to another ROM later, first format data in the PE recovery, then flash the other recovery.
    • Messages about "metadata" or "libfs_mgr" during the first install are normal. Ignore them.
    • No computer cable handy? You can also copy the ROM zip to a FAT32/exFAT/NTFS SD card or an OTG USB stick and choose it in the PE recovery menu.
    • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or sensors not working after the install? The persist partition of this tablet is known to be fragile and can get damaged. The XDA thread of the ROM explains how to restore it.
    • The tablet shows "Press any key to shutdown" when you connect it in fastboot. This is a known problem with USB 3.x ports on Windows. First try a USB 2.0 port (at the back of a desktop, black ones) or put a cheap USB 2.0 hub between the computer and the tablet. Replug the cable after switching the tablet off and on.
    • The tablet shows up as "Android" with a yellow triangle in Windows. Open Device Manager, right-click it, choose Update driver → Browse my computer → Let me pick from a list → Android Device → Android Bootloader Interface. Windows already includes this driver.
    • Last resort for the shutdown message (advanced, Windows only). A community workaround changes some USB settings. Open PowerShell as Administrator and run the three lines, then replug the tablet: 
      reg add "HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\usbflags\18D1D00D0100" /v osvc /t REG_BINARY /d 0000 /f
reg add "HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\usbflags\18D1D00D0100" /v SkipContainerIdQuery /t REG_BINARY /d 01000000 /f
reg add "HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\usbflags\18D1D00D0100" /v SkipBOSDescriptorQuery /t REG_BINARY /d 01000000 /f
      To undo it: reg delete "HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\usbflags\18D1D00D0100" /f
    How to check a SHA-256 checksum (Windows, macOS, Linux)

    A SHA-256 checksum is a 64-character fingerprint of a file. If your downloaded file gives the same fingerprint as the one on the official download page, it is exactly the right file and was not damaged on the way. LineageOS shows the checksum next to each build on its download page.

    Windows (Command Prompt or PowerShell):

    1. Open the Start menu, type cmd and press Enter.
    2. Type certutil -hashfile (with a space at the end), drag the downloaded file into the window so its path appears, then type SHA256 and press Enter.
    certutil -hashfile "D:\Downloads\lineage-24.0-20261015-nightly-flame-signed.zip" SHA256

    The long line of letters and numbers under the file name is the checksum. PowerShell users can run Get-FileHash "path\to\file.zip" -Algorithm SHA256 instead. Large files take a few seconds to a minute.

    macOS (Terminal):

    shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/lineage-24.0-20261015-nightly-flame-signed.zip

    Linux (Terminal):

    sha256sum ~/Downloads/lineage-24.0-20261015-nightly-flame-signed.zip

    Compare it. Capital or small letters do not matter, and spaces inside the number are ignored. Every other character must match exactly. You can also paste the official checksum into the box under the file in the installer and press Verify file. The page compares it for you.

    It does not match? Delete the file, download it again from the official page (not from a mirror or a forum), and check again. If it still does not match, do not flash it.

    Something went wrong during install: what to do

    First, stay calm. The steps this page does (flash dtbo and boot, then sideload the ROM) never touch the bootloader, so you can almost always start over. The page also explains the error in the log box in plain words. Read it before you do anything else, and do not unplug the phone while data is being sent unless the log tells you to.

    • The phone does not show up in the USB list. Use a data cable (some only charge) in a port directly on the computer. Check that the phone shows the bootloader screen (or recovery with Apply from ADB selected). On Windows install Google's USB driver if the phone appears as an unknown device. Then try again.
    • "Another program is using the phone". Open Command Prompt and run adb kill-server. Close Android Studio and any other tab with this page. Replug the cable and try again.
    • The phone stays on the fastboot screen after flashing. Press Volume Up or Down until Recovery mode is shown and press Power. If it comes back to fastboot again, run step 1 again.
    • Recovery says "Signature verification failed". Choose No. The ZIP is damaged or not official. Check its checksum (see the guide above) and download it again.
    • Recovery says "Install completed with status 1" or "2", or "Installation aborted". Usually a damaged file or the wrong stock firmware (this ROM needs Android 13). Choose Advanced → Reboot to recovery, then Apply update → Apply from ADB and sideload again. Advanced → View recovery logs shows the details.
    • "To install additional packages, you need to reboot recovery first". This is normal after a successful install. Choose No, then Reboot system now.
    • The first start takes very long. The first boot after install can take 5 to 10 minutes. Do not unplug or restart before then.
    • The phone is stuck on a logo, restarts again and again, or says it cannot find a valid operating system. Hold Power + Volume Down for the bootloader, open Recovery mode, choose Factory reset → Format data and sideload the ROM again.
    • Nothing responds and the screen is black. Hold Power for 20 to 30 seconds. Then hold Power + Volume Down to reach the bootloader.

    Go back to the original Android. Google's official Android Flash Tool restores a Pixel to factory software from the browser. Factory images are also listed on developers.google.com/android/images. This erases the phone.

    What a successful install looks like
    1. The log shows each step finishing, and the progress bar fills to 100% for dtbo, boot and the ROM.
    2. On the phone, recovery shows Update package verification ... (result 0), then Step 1/2, Step 2/2 and Install completed with status 0.
    3. It may ask To install additional packages, you need to reboot recovery first. Choose No.
    4. Choose Reboot system now. The LineageOS logo appears and the first start takes several minutes. Then the setup screen shows up.
    Browser requirements

    Use Chrome, Edge or another Chromium browser on a computer. Firefox and Safari do not support WebUSB.

    About

    OpenFlash is an open-source catalog of Android ROMs with one guided installer for all of them. Existing web installers each support a single ROM. This project puts many ROMs and many devices in one place. Official builds are marked Official. Unofficial builds are allowed too: they are marked Community and show an extra warning before install.

    Devices are described in the DEVICES list in app.js: the files needed, the flashing steps and the warnings. To add a phone or a ROM, open a pull request with a new entry. See the README. Moving every device into its own JSON file is planned.
